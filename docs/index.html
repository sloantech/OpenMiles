Everything you need for a mileage log

Built for people who drive for work and want accurate records without the monthly bill.

Automatic or manual

Turn on automatic drive detection, or tap Start and Stop yourself. Pause detection for an hour, a day or a week.

Swipe to classify

Swipe right for Business, left for Personal. Medical, Charity and your own categories are a tap away.

Rules that do the work

Name places like Home or Office and set work hours, and new drives get the right category on their own.

IRS rates by date

Mileage value uses the IRS standard rates in effect on each drive's date, including mid-year changes.

Reports you can hand in

Any date range, as a printable PDF mileage log or a spreadsheet. Parking, tolls and yearly odometer readings included.

See every drive on a map

Tap a drive to see the route it took, drawn on your phone with no internet. Want streets? One tap opens it in Google Maps.

Fix anything

Edit, split or merge drives, or add one by hand. Changed drives are clearly marked, so your log stays honest.

Bring your history

Import past drives from your Google Maps Timeline, or the CSV report from your old mileage app. Read only on your phone.

Your data, your backup

Save a backup file to wherever you like and restore it on a new phone. Restoring never deletes anything.

Light and dark

A clean navy and amber look that follows your phone's light or dark setting.

Private by design

Privacy isn't a setting in OpenMiles. It's how the app is built.

  • The app has no internet permission, so it cannot send your drives anywhere. Every build checks this and fails if it ever changes.
  • Location is used only while a drive is recording, and a notification always shows when it is.
  • Background location is only requested if you turn on automatic detection.
  • Nothing leaves your phone unless you tap Export, Share, Backup or Open in Maps, and only to the place you pick.
  • Every build runs security checks and scans every library for known vulnerabilities.

Questions

The honest answers, including the limits.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

It's free, with no paid tier, ads or data sales. It was built by one developer who was tired of paying a monthly fee for mileage tracking. If it helps you, you can leave a donation, but you never have to.

Is my data safe?

The app has no internet permission, so it cannot send your drives anywhere, and every build checks that. Drives stay in the app's private storage on your phone. Nothing leaves unless you export, back up, or tap Open in Maps yourself. See the privacy policy.

Is it accurate enough for taxes?

It records the date, times, miles, category and your notes for each drive, and the PDF log adds totals, IRS-rate values and yearly odometer readings. GPS can still miss or misjudge distance, so compare your first weeks with your odometer. OpenMiles is not tax advice; check with a tax professional.

Will it drain my battery?

With manual Start and Stop, GPS only runs while you're driving. Automatic detection uses Android's low-power activity sensor while you're not driving, and GPS only once a drive starts. Expect some extra use with it on.

What if I lose or replace my phone?

Make a backup in Settings and save it somewhere you choose, like Google Drive or email. Restore it on the new phone. Restoring only adds what's missing and never deletes anything.

How do updates work?

Download the newest version from the release page and it installs over the old one, keeping your drives. The app gently reminds you to check after about two months. If you'd like automatic update alerts, the free Obtainium app can watch the project for you.

Is it on the Play Store? What about iPhone?

Not yet. For now it installs from the project's release page, and Android asks you to allow the install. That's normal for apps outside the Play Store. OpenMiles is Android only for now.

Is it finished?

It's in beta. Every build runs a full set of automatic tests plus security checks, and real-world driving tests are underway. See what changed in each version.

Install it

  1. On your Android phone, open the newest release.
  2. Under Assets, tap the file that ends in .apk.
  3. Open the download and tap Install. Android asks you to allow installs from your browser. That is normal for apps outside the Play Store.
OpenMiles is in beta. It is tested automatically on every build, and real-world driving tests are underway. For your first few weeks, compare it with your odometer or another tracker. See what changed in each version.
