Why OpenMiles

Most mileage apps work well, but you pay every month, and your location history lives on someone else's servers. OpenMiles does the same core job, for free, entirely on your phone.

Everything you need for a mileage log

Built for people who drive for work and want accurate records without the monthly bill.

Automatic or manual

Turn on automatic drive detection, or tap Start and Stop yourself. Pause detection for an hour, a day or a week.

Swipe to classify

Swipe right for Business, left for Personal. Medical, Charity and your own categories are a tap away.

Rules that do the work

Name places like Home or Office and set work hours, and new drives get the right category on their own.

IRS rates by date

Mileage value uses the IRS standard rates in effect on each drive's date, including mid-year changes.

Reports you can hand in

Any date range, as a printable PDF mileage log or a spreadsheet. Parking, tolls and yearly odometer readings included.

Fix anything

Edit, split or merge drives, or add one by hand. Changed drives are clearly marked, so your log stays honest.

Bring your history

Import past drives from your Google Maps Timeline export, read only on your phone.

Your data, your backup

Save a backup file to wherever you like and restore it on a new phone. Restoring never deletes anything.

Light and dark

A clean navy and amber look that follows your phone's light or dark setting.

Private by design

Privacy isn't a setting in OpenMiles. It's how the app is built.

  • The app has no internet permission, so it cannot send your drives anywhere. Every build checks this and fails if it ever changes.
  • Location is used only while a drive is recording, and a notification always shows when it is.
  • Background location is only requested if you turn on automatic detection.
  • Nothing leaves your phone unless you tap Export, Share or Backup, and only to the place you pick.
  • Every build runs security checks and scans every library for known vulnerabilities.

Install it

  1. On your Android phone, open the newest release.
  2. Under Assets, tap the file that ends in .apk.
  3. Open the download and tap Install. Android asks you to allow installs from your browser. That is normal for apps outside the Play Store.
OpenMiles is in beta. It is tested automatically on every build, and real-world driving tests are underway. For your first few weeks, compare it with your odometer or another tracker. See what changed in each version.

Free, with one request

OpenMiles is free software under the GPL-3.0 license. It was built so people don't have to pay a monthly fee to track mileage, so please don't sell it. If it saves you money, you can support the developer instead at paypal.me/joshuasloan.
